Recurring charges are easy to lose track of because they are small, they use different payment methods, and some of them appear only once a year. A practical review starts by finding them, writing them down, and then deciding, one by one, whether each one still earns its place. This guide is a process for doing that. It is not a promise that you will spend less, and it is not advice about which services to keep.

Start a single list

Open a blank page or a simple spreadsheet and give it a few columns you can fill as you go: the name of the charge, how you pay for it, how often it bills, the amount, the next renewal if you can find it, and a short note. You will not have every field on the first pass. The point of the first pass is to catch names.

Include more than streaming apps. Recurring charges can include software, cloud storage, news, memberships, gym or class fees, delivery clubs, domain names, children's apps, and trials that converted to a paid plan.

Check bank and card statements

Look at each account you spend from: checking, credit cards, and any other card or payment app you use. Review several months, not just the current one. A quarterly or annual charge will not show up in a single monthly statement.

Search or scan for familiar merchant names, then also look for charges you do not recognize. Write down the name, the date, the amount, and the account it came from. If the same name appears more than once, note the pattern: weekly, monthly, or something less frequent. If two cards in the same household pay for similar services, check both.

Check app stores and account pages

Phone and computer app stores keep their own subscription lists, separate from the merchant name on a bank statement. Sign in and look at the subscriptions section for each store you use, such as the ones attached to an Apple, Google, Amazon, or Microsoft account.

Also open the account or billing pages of services you already know you use. The plan name, price, and renewal date are often clearer there than on a bank line. If someone else in the household has their own app store account, those subscriptions will not appear on yours.

Search email receipts and calendar reminders

Search your email for words like receipt, subscription, membership, renew, trial, and your payment. Look in promotions and spam folders too. A receipt can confirm a charge you spotted on a statement, or surface one that is billed through a service you forgot.

Some companies send a renewal notice before an annual charge. Those messages are easy to miss, but they often include the date and the amount. If you use a calendar, check it for renewal reminders you set in a previous year.

Look specifically for annual and quiet charges

Annual renewals are easy to undercount because they appear once and then go quiet. While you scan statements, note any charge that showed up only once in the past year and ask whether it will return. Also look for trials that were supposed to end, a plan that changed price at renewal, a family plan that still lists people who no longer use it, and a card you replaced, if a service moved or retried.

You do not have to resolve all of these on the day you find them. Getting them onto the list is the first job.

Review each item once the list is reasonably complete

When the list looks close to complete, go down it and mark a simple decision for each line. A common set of choices is keep, cancel, or change to a cheaper plan.

For each one, a few plain questions help: Do I still use this? Does anyone else in the household use it? Is there a less expensive plan that still covers what we use? When does it renew, and do I need to act before that date?

Write the decision and the date you made it. If you cancel or change a plan, do that in the service's own account page, then watch the next statement to confirm the charge changed. A note on your list is not the same as a completed cancellation. Be cautious with services tied to work, a shared family plan, or stored files.

Keep the list where you can update it

A review is more useful if you can add a new charge when it appears and glance at what renews in the coming month. You can keep the same page and update it, or start a fresh list once a year and compare.
